# Transcreation verification contract

Apply this contract after the writer pass. The verifier must not be the writer.

## Product truth

- Every capability, limitation, comparison, and trust claim is supported by the product source.
- The copy explains the product's real role without turning assistance into authorship.
- The differentiation from a blank document, notes app, generic chat, or standard editor is concrete and defensible.

## Human truth

- The narrative begins with what the person carries: thoughts, memories, experience, knowledge, questions, or a story.
- The person has a reason to express, shape, remember, understand, or pass this material on.
- The product helps the material take shape while the person keeps voice, choices, and ownership.
- The final book or other artifact appears as a possible result, not the only human motive.

## Native language

- High-impact terminology and phrases have an evidence verdict.
- Sentence order, collocations, register, and emotional intensity fit the target language.
- No phrase depends on the reader recognizing the source-language skeleton.
- Interface actions are immediate and clear even when the surrounding narrative is emotional.
- Repeated marketing rhythm across locales has not replaced native expression.

## Semantic coverage

- Every required source meaning is present somewhere appropriate.
- Added meaning is supported and does not inflate the promise.
- Headline compression has not removed the user, problem, action, or outcome.
- The comparison with standard tools says what becomes easier or more coherent, not merely that the product is "better."

## Interface integrity

- Keys, placeholders, variables, links, markup, and plural behavior are intact.
- Copy length still fits its visual job.
- Terminology is consistent across related screens.
- Empty, loading, success, and error states still guide the next action.
- Legal, admin, and excluded namespaces were not changed.

## Independent verifier questions

Answer all ten before passing a locale:

1. What will a native reader think this product is after the first screen?
2. What can the person do next?
3. What human material or intention does the narrative begin from?
4. Who remains the author and decision-maker?
5. Which phrase sounds most translated, even if grammatical?
6. Which source meaning is missing or weakened?
7. Which claim cannot be proven from the product source?
8. Which term is inconsistent, ambiguous, or in the wrong register?
9. Which high-impact phrase has the weakest native evidence?
10. Does the rendered copy still fit and guide action?

## Passing bar

A locale passes only when:

- there are no blocking findings;
- no high-impact claim lacks product evidence;
- source meaning has full coverage;
- the person's purpose and agency are explicit;
- no obvious calque remains;
- mechanical locale checks pass;
- the changed surfaces have been reviewed in their rendered context.

## Change-list format

For each locale provide:

```text
Locale:
Narrative shift:
Terms changed:
Literal phrases rejected:
Surfaces changed:
Native evidence:
Unresolved risks:
Mechanical checks:
Rendered checks:
```
